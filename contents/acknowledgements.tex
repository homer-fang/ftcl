Each stage exposed different engineering problems and helped me understand interpreters, algorithms, and heterogeneous computing more deeply.

I also thank my classmates and friends for discussions about possible thesis directions, including database scripting, probabilistic modeling, reinforcement-learning scheduling, and computational geometry. These discussions helped clarify why the combination of FTCL, UVec, and geometry is a strong direction for this project.

Finally, I thank the open-source communities behind Tcl, CMake, CUDA, LaTeX, and related tools. The reproducible scripts and generated figures in this project are built on top of these tools.
